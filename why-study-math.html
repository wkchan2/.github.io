PHILOSOPHY

What is the point of life if you don't learn?

It is like life: you don't know why you are here, but you are here. I believe you sometimes ask about the meaning of life—can you accept that your life is only study, work, and die, solely for the purpose of something? You have to do something, and mathematics is one of the things you can do. Not everyone can understand math, but I hope I can give you motivation to look at it seriously.

SHORTCUT

Math gives you the shortcut to work smarter

In the book 《數學就是這樣用》, one key concept stands out: mathematics is essentially a collection of shortcuts invented by smart people so they wouldn't have to work as hard.

Instead of counting items one by one, someone invented multiplication. Instead of trying every single possibility in real life, we build algebraic equations to find the exact answer in seconds. Math gives you patterns and algorithms—it is the ultimate mental tool that lets you skip brute-force trial and error.

REALITY CHECK

" I will never use sin, cos, or tan when I buy vegetables, math is useless!!"

A friend once told me a story about his secondary school teacher. A student raised his hand and complained: "Teacher, why do I need to learn trigonometric functions? I won't use sin, cos, or tan when I grow up!"

The teacher casually looked at him and said:

"Trigonometric functions are useful for computer science, engineering, and physics. Don't worry, you won't need to use them because all you'll ever need is basic addition to buy vegetables at the wet market. Work harder, buddy."

The student was left in utter silence. While it sounds harsh, the message is real: advanced math creates software, game engines, architecture, and modern technology. If you don't learn it, you aren't proving math is useless—you are just limiting what you can build in the future.

THE BRIDGE

Do mathematicians live in a parallel universe, or is there a hidden bridge to reality?

It often feels like mathematicians live in a completely different, ideal world compared to normal people. The Big Question always comes up:
Is math's application in real life just a crazy coincidence, or is math built directly into the fabric of our reality?

A friend of mine who loves finding connections between abstract concepts and the real world believes the answer is positive. Math isn't just floating isolated in the abstract; human logic is the key tool we use to uncover the invisible bridge connecting mathematical truth to physical reality.

LITERACY

How does knowing basic math protect your wallet from getting scammed?

You don't need to be a professional mathematician, but you need basic Math Literacy to survive in modern society. Without understanding percentages, compound interest, and basic probability, you won't know how to calculate your salary tax, evaluate investment risks, or protect yourself from predatory financial traps.

Scammers rely on the fact that humans think linearly while money, interest, and risk scale exponentially. Here are a few examples:

1. The "100% Accurate Stock Predictor" Email Scam

A scammer emails 10,000 people. To 5,000, they predict Stock A goes up; to the other 5,000, they predict it goes down. Whichever happens, they throw away the wrong group and repeat this 5 times:
10,000 → 5,000 → 2,500 → 1,250 → 625 → 312 people
To the final ~300 people, the scammer predicted the market with 100% accuracy 5 times in a row—so they gladly pay thousands for the "next secret pick," completely unaware it was just probability tree elimination.

2. The "1% Daily Return" Ponzi Trap

"1% return a day" sounds small and realistic compared to "1000% a year." But thanks to exponential growth, (1 + 0.01)³⁶⁵ = 37.78—that's a 3,678% annual return! An initial $1,000 investment would supposedly become $37,780 in one year and $1.4 Million in two years. Basic compound interest math instantly reveals it as an impossible Ponzi scheme.

3. The Percentage "Dip" Illusion

A crypto coin drops 50% on Monday, then goes up 50% on Tuesday. Are you back to break-even? No! If a $100 coin drops 50%, it becomes $50. A 50% gain on $50 is only $25, leaving you at $75 (a net 25% loss). Scammers rely on changing base values to make bad deals look great.

"Learning math isn't just about passing exams—it is literally self-defense for your wallet."
MINDSET FOR BEGINNERS

i am not a math person... i cannot learn it well

Don't give up just yet!!!Many people think being "good at math" means doing hundreds of practice questions, memorizing formulas, and finishing exam papers as fast as possible. If you struggle with math, this high-quantity approach usually leads straight to burnout and feeling stupid.

The secret is that math is about quality and quantity,quality of understanding, quantity of exercise:

without understanding you cannot solve the problem, without exercise you will forget the solving method quickly.

  • 1 Deep Problem > 50 Rushed Exercises: It is infinitely better to spend an hour truly understanding why one single equation works than mindlessly solving 50 exercises using formulas you don't understand.
  • Focus on Concepts, Not Calculation: Calculators do the quantity (arithmetic); humans do the quality (logic). If you struggle with numbers, focus on the big ideas and logical connections behind them instead.
  • Progress at Your Own Pace: Math is built like Lego blocks—if a foundation is shaky, everything above it falls. Taking time to master one concept deeply builds a strong foundation that makes future math much easier.
"Math is not about how many questions you solve, but how deeply you understand the few you do."
PERSPECTIVE

Is doing math equations really just weightlifting for your brain?

When people go to the gym and lift heavy dumbbells, they don't do it because they plan to lift heavy rocks at their daily job. They do it to build overall strength.

Math is weightlifting for your brain. Even if you never solve a quadratic equation again in your adult life, the process of solving it trains your brain in logical deduction, step-by-step reasoning, and remaining calm when faced with complex, unfamiliar problems.
