Things to Tell You: An Open Letter to My 12-Year-Old Self

Reflections from a 22-Year-Old Math Teacher

Before teaching mathematics, I want to share the life lessons, habits, and perspectives that shaped who I am today.

Pillar 1

Building vs. Consuming

Video games can be fun, but do not let pure consumption take over your life. Learn piano, play chess, edit videos, or code. Real skills stay with you forever.

Protect your eyes and body early. Screens and poor posture catch up with you faster than you expect.

Move consistently through running, cycling, or core exercises—not only for competition, but for long-term health and mental clarity.

Pillar 2

Living with Others

Listen to your parents, even when you disagree. Their advice usually comes from care and may save you from unnecessary detours.

Let go of resentment and try not to hate. Holding onto anger takes away your own peace. Learn to forgive early.

Surround yourself with people who inspire you to grow, and express gratitude to those who support you.

Pillar 3

Mindset & How to Think

Focus on your trajectory rather than your starting point. Consistent daily effort compounds over time.

Protect your attention span. Creating things gives energy back; passive scrolling slowly drains it.

Accept being a beginner. The fastest way to improve is to tolerate looking bad without letting ego stop you.

Focus on what you can control and let go of what you cannot.

Pillar 4

Living with Yourself

Adults do not have everything figured out either. You do not need a perfectly linear life plan right now.

You are allowed to outgrow hobbies, relationships, and mindsets without bitterness.

Treat yourself kindly on difficult days. Your inner voice gradually forms your self-image.

A Final Note

You will make mistakes, and that is a natural part of growing. You do not need to have everything solved immediately. Keep learning, keep moving forward, and remember that progress matters more than perfection.
