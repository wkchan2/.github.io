Historical Incident Igor Tamm

The Taylor Series at Gunpoint

During the Russian Civil War, physicist and mathematician Igor Tamm was captured by anti-communist bandits. Suspecting him of being an anti-bandit spy, the leader drew a gun and asked for his profession. When Tamm claimed to be a mathematician, the leader demanded:

"Calculate the error introduced when a Taylor series is truncated at the n-th term. If you get it right, you live. If you fail, I shoot you."

Trembling under gunpoint, Tamm derived the formula in the dirt with a stick. The bandit leader—who turned out to be a former math student—inspected the proof, nodded, and set him free.

Rivalry Isaac Newton

Newton’s Rigged Investigation

Did you know that Isaac Newton was incredibly arrogant and thought no girl was qualified to be his girlfriend because he thought everyone was stupid?

When Isaac Newton and Gottfried Leibniz disputed who invented calculus first, the Royal Society formed an "impartial committee" to investigate and issue an official report.

The report completely ruined Leibniz’s reputation and declared Newton the sole creator. The catch? Newton secretly wrote the entire report himself, appointed his own friends to the committee, and even published an anonymous review praising his own paper!

Millennium Prize Grigori Perelman

The Hermit Who Declined a Million Dollars

In 2002, Russian mathematician Grigori Perelman solved the Poincaré Conjecture, a legendary problem that had stumped mathematicians for over a century.

When offered both the Fields Medal and the $1,000,000 Millennium Prize, Perelman refused both. Declaring that the official math community was unethical, he abandoned academia completely and chose to live quietly in Saint Petersburg, gathering mushrooms in the forest and playing violin with his mother.

Tragedy Georg Cantor

The Architect of Infinities

Georg Cantor proved that some infinities are strictly larger than others, fundamentally revolutionizing set theory. However, contemporary mathematicians ridiculed his work, calling him a "corrupter of youth" and a scientific charlatan.

Overwhelmed by relentless hostility and deep clinical depression, Cantor spent his final years in sanatoriums, dying isolated in a mental hospital. Decades later, David Hilbert famously remarked: "No one shall expel us from the paradise that Cantor has created."

Group Theory Évariste Galois

The Midnight Genius

At just 20 years old, French mathematician Évariste Galois invented Group Theory. After getting entangled in a political and romantic rivalry, he was challenged to a duel at dawn.

Knowing he was likely to die, Galois spent his entire final night feverishly scribbling his mathematical discoveries onto paper, writing "I have no time!" in the margins. He was killed the next morning, but his last-minute notes transformed abstract algebra forever.

Famous Margin Pierre de Fermat

The Ultimate Historical Troll

In 1637, Pierre de Fermat wrote his famous equation (an + bn = cn) in the margin of a textbook, adding:

“I have discovered a truly marvelous proof of this, which this margin is too narrow to contain.”

He died without ever recording the proof, leading mathematicians on a 358-year wild goose chase until Andrew Wiles finally solved it in 1994 using advanced modern techniques Fermat could never have known.
